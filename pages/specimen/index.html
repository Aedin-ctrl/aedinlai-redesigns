Measure Leading

I build the measuring end of things — instrumentation that has to be right, and the software that makes sense of what it reads. The appeal is not the cleverness available at that end of a system; it is that the work is checkable. Either the reading is the reading that happened, or it is not.

A number that is quietly wrong is worse than no number at all. Everything downstream inherits it, every chart drawn from it looks confident, and nobody can tell by looking. Most of the job is making sure that cannot happen, and almost none of that work shows afterwards.

Where that has gone

In 2025, a season on data centre infrastructure at SingleStore, where a private cloud platform went from 160 virtual machines to over 300 — the count is not the interesting part, the tooling written along the way is. The same year, research hardware built with the Yale School of Medicine and the PEACE Foundation for a neurology study, co-authored and published in JAMA. Before that, an arcade machine built from nothing, including the cabinet.

Both lights at once

I fence épée, which is the one weapon where both lamps can come on together. Once the first fires, the box gives the other forty milliseconds and then it has made up its mind. Four years of that at national level, and this season as captain at Northeastern, taking the team into the national championship.
